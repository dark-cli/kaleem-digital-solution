The problem

Money moving fast, through many hands, with no memory.

Collectors handled cash; some managed accounts; some did both. Wallets were shared, approvals were verbal. When something looked off, nobody could answer: who changed that, when, and from what?

What they asked us for
A permanent history Every transaction, kept for good. Nothing editable after the fact.
Fine-grained permissions A team posts for its own clients and can't even see another team's wallet.
Works with no signal A mobile app for the field that syncs when it reconnects.
Owned outright Full source code. No SaaS, no vendor to depend on.

06 · Permissions

The part nobody thinks about until they need it.

  1. Wallet-wide — who adds members, renames, edits the rules.
  2. Team to team — "Sellers Admin" runs Sellers, not Finance.
  3. Contact groups — which teams can file clients where.
  4. Action-level — read, create, update, delete, close: per team, per group.

Every permission change is itself a recorded event.

07 · Beyond the audit log

Servers that check each other.

Several servers compare cryptographic fingerprints of their event history. To forge a record you'd have to rewrite every server and every device at once, and even then every node raises the alarm, stops taking new events and alerts the admins. The apps keep working offline until the servers agree.

Available in our custom builds.
08 · Going live

How we switched without putting their business at risk.

Why not just switch over? A one-day switch is a bet: if anything breaks on day one, collections stop. Running both systems took the bet off the table and made launch day a non-event.
1 Move the data A migration script copied everything out of the old database and reshaped it for the new system.
2 Run both, side by side For the first days in production the old system stayed on. If anything looked wrong (a bug, a mismatch, a case we hadn't seen), staff could go straight back to it while we fixed it.
3 Switch the old one off Nobody ever needed to fall back. The old system was retired, and Deptmaster runs the business.
