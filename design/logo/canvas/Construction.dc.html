Out of the box, by the numbers

Others put you in a box. We walk you out of it. The frame is the box, the open corner is the door, the yellow block is you, mid-step. Every size below comes from one rule: each part is the one before it divided by φ. On a grid of 110 those parts are Fibonacci numbers.

The wall is cut at the golden section. Each open edge is 55 of wall and 34 of door: 0.618 to 0.382, the classic division of a line.

The block is exactly as wide as the door. You fit through the opening you were given, no more, no less.

It stands on the threshold. The block has stepped out 21, so 13 of it is still inside, exactly the wall's thickness: its back edge lines up with the inner face of both walls (34 − 21 = 13). Out, but only just: the moment of leaving.

Mirror it across the diagonal and nothing changes. Both gaps are 21, both wall runs 55, and the block's centre sits on the axis.

The whole mark is a square, 110 × 110 (89 + 21), so it sits on the lockup's square without padding tricks.
